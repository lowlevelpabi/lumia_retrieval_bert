**Actual Section:** [ Introduction ]

> Emergencies, whether resulting from natural disasters, accidents, or humaninduced hazards, continue to pose significant threats to human life, property, and the environment. Effective emergency management requires rapid response, accurate information dissemination, efficient resource mobilization, and seamless coordination among responders. However, many existing emergency response systems are plagued with critical inefficiencies, including slow and inaccurate incident reporting, delayed response times, poor real-time communication, and the absence of reliable rating system. Despite efforts by local government units and emergency services to mitigate risks through structural assessments, monitoring, and preparedness programs (Elayda et al., 2023), substantial gaps remain. In particular, there is a noticeable lack of systems that actively engage civilians, provide real-time incident reporting, and ensure immediate access to emergency services. The inability of current systems to offer userfriendly platforms for reporting emergencies, locating nearby emergency departments, and tracking response progress further exacerbates these challenges. Several studies have emphasized the importance of enhancing emergency response systems through better resource allocation, real-time data sharing, and improved coordination (Shen et al., 2004; Bongiovanni et al., 2017; Nuţă et al., 2015; Blasch et al., 2019). However, the persistent problems of fragmented communication, delayed response times, and insufficient public participation highlight the urgent need for a more integrated and technologically advanced solution. Recognizing these pressing issues, the researchers proposed the development of ALERTO: Enhancing Emergency Response and Reporting Using Real-Time Data Processing and Geospatial Tracking Algorithms. This project aims to address existing gaps by introducing a mobile-based application designed to streamline emergency reporting, enhance geospatial tracking, and facilitate real-time coordination between civilians and emergency responders. And also, the system ensures accessibility and user-friendliness, and includes key features to improve functionality and trust. These include secure account authentication through OTP (One-Time Password) verification, real-time notifications to alert administrators and responders immediately upon new incident reports, and an estimated responder arrival time display, allowing users to stay informed and reassured while waiting for help. ALERTO leverages modern technologies by utilizing Firebase for real-time database management, integrating Google Maps API for accurate geospatial tracking, and being developed on the Android platform using the Java programming language. The system is designed to be accessible, user-friendly, and capable of providing ratings to the respondent, thereby improving the overall efficiency and responsiveness of emergency services. The development of ALERTO reflects the researchers’ strong desire and concern to improve current emergency management practices. By harnessing mobile technologies, the project aspires to create a safer and more responsive emergency service network, enhancing civilian engagement, optimizing resource coordination, and ultimately minimizing casualties and property damage during emergencies.

By 2021, Excelsa made up to 7% of the total global production and Liberica has 1%. The Philippines’ lowland and mountain regions made the country suitable for the four varieties of coffee, with Excelsa and Liberica as a rare commodity which made the country different to the most coffee producing countries (Dizon, 2021). Coffee plants in the Philippines have several diseases including coffee leaf rust, sooty mold, brown eye spot, and coffee berry disease. Coffee plant leaves in National Coffee Research, Development and Extension Center (NCRDEC) like Coffea liberica var. dewevrei and Coffea canephora were mostly infested with Coffee Leaf Rust (CLR) and Cercospora Leaf Spots (CLS), sometimes called Brown Eye Spots. In comparison to the amount of CLR and CLS infected plant leaves, other types of disease have limited or not enough data for model training. Like Madhurya & Jubilson (2024) said, most plant diseases have observable symptoms and the widely used method in identifying plant leaf disease is by visually examining the affected plant leaves. The manual examination is too much labor and prone to human error as such, the demand to develop an effective and reliable approach that automatically detects and locates such various diseases on coffee plant leaves (Nawaz et al., 2024). “The detection of plant disease objects plays an essential role within the realm of plant protection” (X. Wang & Liu, 2024). Thus, the objective was to develop an approach that detects C. canephora (Robusta) and C. liberica var. dewevrei (Excelsa) plant leaf diseases, CLR and CLS using a Deep Learning based Computer Vision, You Only Look Once (YOLO) architecture. The popularity of using machine learning for plant leaf disease detection has been rising and has become common in recent years. The YOLO architecture is an image object detection algorithm known for its speed and accuracy. The continuous development and open access to code and weight made YOLO a popular choice among developers and researchers (Nelson, 2024). Early plant leaf detection and identification is possible using the YOLO Deep learning technique with a high accuracy of 99.69% (Madhurya & Jubilson, 2024). The primary aim of such a model is for early detection, which is essential to avoid spreading and financial losses for the farmers. However, most existing training datasets for coffee plant disease typically feature a single background image in laboratory settings. This limitation will make it difficult for the model to adapt to recognition tasks under realworld complex conditions. Therefore, the objective was to enhance the datasets for Robusta and Excelsa plant leaf disease and use them for training and evaluation of their proposed model. Additionally, He et al., (2024) demonstrated the possibility of the YOLO model being optimized for mobile-embedded devices. The proposed model, YOLOv10, will be utilized and enhanced to be suitable for mobile embedded terminals, thus allowing it to be deployed as a mobile application. YOLOv10 was developed by researchers from the Tsinghua University. YOLOv10, like its other predecessor, outperforms them in terms of accuracy and its YOLOv10-N variant has reduced computational overhead and latency by 70%. YOLOv10 sets a new industry standard for object detection models making it highly suitable for applications on mobile devices (Dupont, 2024).

Summary The study entitled “Deepfake Detection of Known Personalities and Public Figures using CNN Algorithm” aimed to develop a web-based application capable of detecting AI-generated media and identifying known Filipino celebrities using deep learning techniques. In detecting deepfakes the system utilized a pretrained Xception model, which was fine-tuned using a curated deepfake dataset. For celebrity face recognition, MTCNN was employed for accurate face detection, while FaceNet handled facial embedding and recognition. Once a celebrity is recognized, SERP API is used to web scrape and return the top three matching results for additional verification and context. The application was developed using Python, with key libraries including TensorFlow/Keras, and deployed using Streamlit. The environment used for model development and testing was Google Colab. The Agile methodology was adopted for its iterative and adaptive nature, making it ideal for a system dealing with the fast-paced evolution of deepfake technology. This allowed for continuous integration, testing, and improvements throughout the development cycle. In evaluating the system's performance, the researchers used the ISO 9126 quality model. Summary The study entitled “Deepfake Detection of Known Personalities and Public Figures using CNN Algorithm” aimed to develop a web-based application capable of detecting AI-generated media and identifying known Filipino celebrities using deep learning techniques. The system integrates three core functionalities: deepfake detection, face recognition, and web scraping to enhance digital media verification and combat misinformation online. In detecting deepfakes the system utilized a pretrained Xception model, which was fine-tuned using a curated deepfake dataset. For celebrity face recognition, MTCNN was employed for accurate face detection, while FaceNet handled facial embedding and recognition. Once a celebrity is recognized, SERP API is used to web scrape and return the top three matching results for additional verification and context. The application was developed using Python, with key libraries including TensorFlow/Keras, and deployed using Streamlit. The environment used for model development and testing was Google Colab. The Agile methodology was adopted for its iterative and adaptive nature, making it ideal for a system dealing with the fast-paced evolution of deepfake technology. This allowed for continuous integration, testing, and improvements throughout the development cycle. In evaluating the system's performance, the researchers used the ISO 9126 quality model. An evaluation survey was conducted with thirty (30) respondents: five (5) IT experts, five (5) journalism professionals, and twenty (20) social media users. These user groups were chosen based on their relevance, IT experts for their expertise in machine learning and system architecture, Journalists as fact-checkers who can benefit from deepfake verification tools, Social media users being the most vulnerable to AI-generated scams and misinformation. The evaluation assessed the system's functionality (3.9), reliability (4.0), usability (4.2), efficiency (3.9), and portability (4.3), resulting in a grand mean of 4.1, interpreted as “Very Good”. This indicates the system performs well across all essential quality metrics. The highest rating was recorded for portability, suggesting the system works effectively across various platforms, while functionality received the lowest score, indicating a potential area for enhancement. Despite this, the overall results validate the system’s utility and user satisfaction. This digitized approach significantly reduces the time and effort needed for manual verification and helps prevent the dissemination of false or misleading content involving public figures.
